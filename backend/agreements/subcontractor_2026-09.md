# Independent Contractor Agreement

**Between The Maine Cleaning Co. ("the Company") and you ("the Contractor").**

This is the agreement you accept before taking work through BrightBase. Read it
before you tap Accept. If anything here does not match what you were told, stop
and ask — do not accept it.

---

## 1. You are an independent contractor, not an employee

You run your own cleaning business. The Company is a customer of that business.

That means the Company does **not**:

- withhold income tax, Social Security or Medicare from what it pays you
- provide health insurance, paid time off, sick pay, or retirement benefits
- pay unemployment insurance or workers' compensation premiums on your behalf
- control your hours, or require you to be available

You are responsible for your own taxes, your own insurance, and your own
business. Section 7 covers taxes and Section 6 covers insurance.

Nothing in this agreement makes either of us the other's employee, partner,
agent, or joint venturer. Neither of us can make promises on the other's
behalf.

## 2. How work is offered — and why nothing is ever assigned to you

The Company posts jobs. You look at what is posted and decide whether to ask
for any of it.

- **You are never assigned a job.** A posted job is an offer, not an
  instruction.
- **You can decline anything, for any reason or none**, and you never have to
  explain. Declining does not count against you and does not affect what you
  are offered next.
- **You can propose your own price.** If a job is posted at a price you do not
  want, name your own. The Company can accept it or not.
- The Company decides which request to accept when more than one person asks
  for the same job. Until the Company accepts your request in writing (in
  BrightBase), you have no obligation and neither does it.
- **You are not required to accept any minimum amount of work**, or to be
  available at any particular time. If you tell BrightBase when you are usually
  free, that is information to help you see relevant jobs — it is not a
  schedule, and it does not commit you to anything.

Once you accept a job and the Company accepts your request, you have agreed to
do that job. Section 11 covers what happens if you cannot.

## 3. How you are paid

**You are paid an agreed amount per job. You are not paid by the hour.**

- The price for each job is whatever you and the Company agree before the job —
  either the posted price you accepted, or the price you proposed and the
  Company accepted.
- That price does not change because the job took longer or less time than
  expected. If a job turns out to be substantially different from how it was
  described, tell the Company before continuing and you can agree a new price
  or you can decline the job.
- Payment is made within **15 days** of the job being completed.
- If you use helpers (Section 5), you pay them. The Company pays you, and only
  you, for the whole job.
- Travel to and from jobs is your cost and is included in the price you agreed.

## 4. You control how the work is done

You decide the methods, the order, the technique, and the sequence of your
work. The Company can tell you **what result it needs** — a clean house, by a
particular day, meeting a customer's stated requirements — but not how you
achieve it.

You supply your own equipment and supplies unless you and the Company agree
otherwise in writing for a specific job. Where a customer requires a particular
product be used or avoided in their home, the Company will tell you before you
accept the job.

The Company does not require you to attend meetings or training, wear a
uniform, or report your hours.

## 5. You can bring your own helpers

You may use employees, helpers or subcontractors of your own to perform work
under this agreement. If you do:

- you hire them, you pay them, and you supervise their work
- you are responsible for their work as if it were your own
- you are responsible for their taxes and their insurance
- you must tell the Company in advance who will be entering a customer's home,
  and anyone entering must meet the same insurance and background requirements
  that apply to you

## 6. Insurance you must carry

Before you can take work, and for as long as you take work, you must maintain
at your own cost:

- **General liability insurance** of at least **$1,000,000 per occurrence** and
  **$2,000,000 aggregate**, and
- either **workers' compensation coverage** for yourself and anyone you bring,
  or a valid **Maine predetermination or exemption** from that requirement

You must give the Company a current certificate of insurance and keep it
current. **If your certificate lapses, you cannot take work until it is
replaced.** BrightBase will stop you from claiming jobs automatically; that is
not a penalty, it is the insurance requirement.

## 7. Taxes

You are responsible for all federal, state and local taxes on what you are
paid, including self-employment tax. Nothing is withheld.

You must give the Company a completed **Form W-9**. The Company will issue you
a **Form 1099-NEC** for any year in which it pays you $600 or more.

## 8. You are free to work for anyone else

This agreement is **not exclusive**. You may work for other cleaning companies,
take your own private clients, and advertise your own business, at any time,
including while you have work in progress for the Company. You do not need to
tell the Company, and it will not affect what you are offered.

The only limit is Section 10, which is about the Company's own customers.

## 9. Doing the work

You are responsible for completing each job you accept, to a professional
standard, on the agreed date.

If work is not completed or falls materially below a professional standard, the
Company may ask you to correct it at your own cost within a reasonable time. If
it is not corrected, the Company may have it corrected and deduct the
reasonable cost from what it owes you, or decline to pay for that job. The
Company will tell you what the problem is and give you a chance to put it right
before doing either.

## 10. Customer information, access, and not going around the Company

Working through the Company gives you access to customers' homes, their
addresses, their gate codes, their alarm codes, their keys, and their habits.
That information is confidential and belongs to the Company and its customers.

You agree that you will:

- use access information only to do the job it was given to you for
- never share it, photograph it, or store it outside BrightBase
- never keep a customer's key or code after a job is finished, unless the
  Company has arranged otherwise in writing
- never enter a customer's property except to do a job you have accepted

**Non-solicitation.** For **12 months** after your last job for the Company,
you will not directly solicit cleaning work from a customer whose home you
entered through the Company, and you will not accept such work if it is offered
to you as a direct result of that introduction. This does not stop you from
general advertising, and it does not stop you from working for someone who was
already your customer before the Company introduced you.

If a customer asks you to work for them directly, tell the Company. It will not
be held against you.

## 11. If you cannot do a job you accepted

Tell the Company as soon as you know — through BrightBase, by phone, or by
text. There is no penalty for telling the Company early. There is a real
problem, for the customer and for the Company, in not being told.

If you do not attend a job you accepted and did not tell the Company, the
Company may decline to offer you further work.

If the Company cancels a job you had accepted with less than 24 hours' notice
and not because of anything you did, it will pay you **50%** of the agreed
price for that job.

## 12. How this ends

Either of us may end this agreement at any time, for any reason, with written
notice — an email or a message in BrightBase is enough.

Ending it does not affect:

- payment for work you have already completed, which is still owed to you
- jobs you have already accepted and not yet done, unless you and the Company
  agree to cancel them
- Section 10, which continues for the period stated there

The Company may suspend your access to jobs immediately, without ending this
agreement, if your insurance lapses, your documents expire, or there is a
credible safety or theft concern. It will tell you why.

## 13. Responsibility for loss or damage

You are responsible for loss or damage you or your helpers cause to a
customer's property, to the extent it was caused by your negligence or by
failing to do what this agreement requires. That is what the insurance in
Section 6 is for.

You agree to cover the Company for claims, losses and reasonable costs arising
from your work, your helpers, your taxes, or your failure to keep the insurance
in Section 6. The Company agrees to do the same for you in respect of its own
acts, its own employees, and information it gave you that turned out to be
wrong.

Neither of us is responsible to the other for indirect or consequential losses,
such as lost profits.

## 14. Maine law

This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Maine, and any dispute
will be brought in the courts of the State of Maine.

## 15. The whole agreement

This document is the entire agreement between us about this work, and it
replaces anything said or written before it.

Changes must be in writing. If the Company updates this agreement, you will be
asked to read and accept the new version, and you can keep working under this
one until you do — the Company will tell you the date by which the new version
takes effect.

If any part of this agreement is found to be unenforceable, the rest still
applies.

1. You have read this agreement.
2. You are running your own business and want to be engaged as an independent
   contractor, not as an employee.
3. The information you have given the Company about your business, your
   insurance and your taxes is true.

Your acceptance is recorded with the date, the version of this document, and a
fingerprint of its exact text, so both of us can always tell which version you
agreed to.
